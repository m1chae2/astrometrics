# Stacking pre-processing

This stage checks the frames and calibration data before any pixels are combined. It does not stack anything. It decides which frames go in and whether the calibration data can be trusted.

Terms are defined in the [glossary](../../../../documentation/Glossary.md). This document also defines each term the first time it appears.

## What this stage does, in order

These terms appear throughout:

- A *light frame* is one camera exposure of the target. *Stacking* averages many aligned light frames into one image with less noise.
- *Calibration frames* are frames that measure the camera rather than the sky. A *bias* frame is a zero-length exposure (the camera's fixed electronic offset). A *dark* is taken with the shutter closed (the signal heat adds over the exposure). A *flat* is an exposure of an evenly lit surface (how sensitive each pixel is). A *master* is the average of many such frames.
- The *gain setting* is the camera's amplifier setting (a unitless number in the FITS `GAIN` card). *Offset* is a constant the camera adds to every pixel so that noise below zero is still recorded. *Binning* is the combining of neighboring pixels (for example 2 by 2) into one.
- *Siril* is the external program that builds the masters and stacks the frames.
- A *session* is the set of light frames of one camera, filter and exposure length taken less than 4 h apart. The frame-quality check judges each session on its own.

Before the numbered checks, the stage keeps the stack to one camera (`pipelines/shared/camera_selection.py`). Frames from two cameras differ in pixel size, gain, noise and calibration frames, so a stack that mixes them has no single meaning. `choose_camera_frames` picks a named camera's frames for `ProcessingPipelines.stack`. `ensure_single_camera` stops the stage with an error when the frames still come from more than one camera. Frames with no camera recorded do not count.

The stage also keeps the stack to one optic (`pipelines/shared/optic_selection.py`). A camera used behind two telescopes or lenses makes frames at two image scales, so a stack that mixes them has no single pixel scale. An optic is known by its focal length in whole millimeters. `choose_optic_frames` picks the frames at one focal length when `ProcessingPipelines.stack` is given `focal_length_mm`. `ensure_single_optic` stops the stage with an error when the frames still come from more than one optic. Frames with no focal length recorded do not count.

1. Gain check (`frame_homogeneity.py`). Frames taken at different camera gain settings have different read noise, so mixing them hurts the stack. The check keeps the most common gain and sets the others aside.
2. Frame-quality check (`frame_quarantine.py`). It measures each light frame and moves a frame with clouds, trailed stars or a pointing far off the session's field into an `_excluded` folder beside it. It skips spectral frames. [The frame-quality check](#the-frame-quality-check) describes it.
3. Background check (`background_homogeneity.py`). It compares each frame's sky background with the others of the same exposure length. It sets aside a frame taken through cloud or twilight (partly measured; see [STK-04](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-04)). `shared/quality/background_measurement.py` measures the sky.
4. Flat check (`flat_calibration.py`). It first looks at every flat frame for clipping: a frame is clipped when more than 0.1% of the pixels of one color channel (of the whole frame, for a monochrome sensor) sit at the saturation level. The stage leaves clipped frames out of the master flat and says so in a warning (design estimate; see [STK-05](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-05)). A set clipped in every frame is kept whole, with a warning. The measurements below then describe the frames that are kept. It measures how many flats there are, how bright they are, and how noisy the master flat would be. It measures brightness against the camera profile's clip ceiling when the camera is known, and against a 16-bit guess otherwise. The clip ceiling is the highest raw count a saturated pixel reaches. A noisy master gets a blur width, which Siril applies as a Gaussian blur (partly measured; see [STK-05](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-05)). The check measures but does not blur the flat of a color sensor, which has a grid of red, green and blue filters over its pixels.
5. Unchanged-stack check (`stack_inputs.py`). It builds a record of these inputs:
    - the frames;
    - the calibration frames the library could pick for them (every temperature and binning of the matching camera, gain, offset and exposure, so a new dark never goes unnoticed);
    - the settings that change a stack's pixels;
    - the stacking-code version.

    It compares the record's hash (a short fingerprint of the record) with the one saved next to the stack on disk. When they match, the stage does not rebuild the stack. The [stacking README](../README.md#skipping-an-unchanged-stack-step-2) gives the rules and how to force a rebuild. A test scans the stacking code for every configuration setting it reads. A new setting has to be classified in `stack_inputs.py` (changes the stack, or does not) before the tests pass.
6. Frame-count check (`assess_input_quality.py`, `calibration_gates`). It reads the `calibration_blocking_flags` that the Siril driver recorded for each master bias, dark or flat built from fewer frames than `minimum_calibration_frames` (default 3; design estimate; see [stacking design estimates](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-35)). Each one goes into the `calibration_frame_count` gate, which fails. The stack is still built.
7. Calibration-match check (`assess_input_quality.py`, `calibration_gates`). It reads two lists from the Siril driver. `calibration_mismatch_flags` records that a dark, bias or flat of another gain or offset, or a dark from another temperature, was applied. `calibration_match_blocking_flags` records that a kind of calibration frame was left out because its binning differs from the lights. `calibration_metadata_reasons` turns each list into one sentence that quotes every entry. The `calibration_metadata` gate fails when either list has an entry. A refused calibration fails the gate even when no frame of that kind was applied. The gate is `not_checked` only when nothing was applied and nothing was refused.
8. Input counts (`assess_input_quality.py`, `record_input_counts`). It stores on the stack's metrics how many frames each check set aside and how many different frames built the master dark and bias.

### The frame-quality check

The check works on a session. It measures each frame with `shared/quality/raw_frame_check.py` and judges the frame against its session.

- **Few stars (cloud or heavy trailing).** A frame with fewer detected stars than half the session median moves (partly measured; see [STK-01](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-01)). The reason names the star count and the sky level. A sky level more than 1.15 times the median reads as cloud, and a normal sky level reads as trailing (partly measured; see [STK-01](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-01)). The 1.15 changes only the wording of the reason, not whether the frame moves. The stored kind for that case is `trailed`, so `trailed` means stars smeared or far fewer than usual under a normal sky. The kind is kept as stored, and the Image Processing Display words it that way.
- **Elongated stars (trailing).** A frame moves when its star elongation is above 1.32 and at least 0.12 above the session median (0.22 for a color camera until more color sessions are stored). `set_aside_elongation_limit` gives that threshold for a session. Elongation is a star's long width divided by its short width (1.0 for a circle). It is the frame's stored `frame.star_elongation`, made by the frame measurement from the full second moments of isolated stars, so a trail at any angle counts. A frame with no stored value is measured on the spot with the same code, which takes seconds. The reason reads like "Stars trailed: elongation 1.64, limit 1.32". A session that is mildly elongated throughout keeps its mildly elongated frames. These limits are partly measured (see [STK-01](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-01), and [STK-02](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-02) for color cameras).
- **Kept but stretched.** A stacked frame at or above the warning level `STRETCHED_STAR_WARNING_ELONGATION` (1.2, design estimate; see WAY-D07 in the [wayfinding design estimates](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/wayfinding.md#design-estimates)) but not above its session's threshold stays in the stack. The frame status (`../frame_status.py`) calls it `flagged`, with both numbers in the reason.
- **Uneven sky (thin cloud).** The check measures the sky in a 4 by 4 grid of boxes, leaving stars out. It divides each box by the frame's own sky level and takes the root-mean-square difference from the session's median pattern, which holds the vignetting and the steady sky gradient. A frame moves when that difference is 5 robust spreads above the session's usual one and at least 0.01. The reason reads like "Uneven sky, likely cloud: 0.043, limit 0.010". The value is stored as `frame.sky_unevenness` (partly measured; see [STK-D72](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#design-estimates)).
- **Pointing off the field.** The check matches the brightest stars of each frame with those of the session's middle frame, as the frame is and turned half a revolution, so a meridian flip does not count as a move. A frame whose center lies more than 0.10 of the frame width from the session's main field moves, because a stack of it would be cut down to the shared area. The reason reads like "Pointing off the main field: 0.24 of the field width, limit 0.10". The value is stored as `frame.pointing_offset_fraction` (partly measured; see [STK-D73](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#design-estimates)).
- **Left alone.** A satellite or aircraft streak leaves the median elongation and star count unchanged, so that frame stays. The stacker's pixel rejection removes the streak. Pixel rejection throws out a pixel's value in one frame when it disagrees strongly with the other frames.
- **Safeguards.** The check does not judge a session of fewer than 8 frames (partly measured; see [STK-01](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-01)). The check sets aside every frame that fails a rule, however many. When more than a quarter of a session is set aside, it still moves them and warns, for example "35 of 123 frames set aside: 4 trailed, 12 off field, 19 uneven sky". The warning goes to the gate record, the log and each frame's entry in `excluded_frames.json` (key `warning`). If every frame of a stack fails, nothing is stacked and the job says why (partly measured; see [STK-03](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-03)). Frames that cannot be read stay where they are.
- **Undoing it.** The check moves frames and never deletes them. `excluded_frames.json` in each `_excluded` folder records the original path, the kind (`clouded`, `trailed` or `off_field`), the reason (the rule, the frame's value and the limit), the measurements and the time. the internal function `restore_excluded_frames` moves them back, and `scripts/restore_excluded_frames.py` is its command-line form. `TargetFrames.restore(target, frames)` moves only the chosen frames back (it passes `only_files` to `restore_quarantined_frames`) and rescans the target; the File Browser's Put back button calls it. `TargetFrames.get_set_aside` lists them, with a flag for any frame that has no manifest entry and so cannot be restored automatically. `ProcessingPipelines.predict_stack` shows what the check would move for a target without moving it: it runs the same frame checks a stack runs, in the same order. Frame scanning and incremental downloads treat `_excluded` as part of the target's folder, so a moved frame does not return to the target or download again.
- **Setting.** `quarantine_bad_frames_enabled` in `[Processing.Siril]`, on by default. Measuring takes about 1 s per frame, plus several seconds for a frame with no stored star elongation.

The limits are in `frame_quarantine.py`.

## Settings

| Setting | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `quarantine_bad_frames_enabled` in `[Processing.Siril]` | on | Turns the frame-quality check off |
| `minimum_calibration_frames` in `[Processing.Siril]` | 3 (design estimate; see [stacking design estimates](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#design-estimates)) | Fewest frames per master before the frame-count check flags the stack |
| Frame-quality limits in `frame_quarantine.py` | 0.5 times the median star count; elongation 1.32 and 0.12 above the median (0.22 color); stretched-star warning 1.2; sky unevenness 0.01; pointing 0.10 of the frame width; 8 frames; warning above a quarter of a session set aside (partly measured; see [STK-01](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-01) and [STK-03](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-03)) | Decide which frames move to `_excluded`, and when to warn |

## Quality metrics

| Field | Unit | Limit (status) | High or low means | Read by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `framesSubmitted`, `framesAccepted` | Frames | None | How many frames the stage got, and how many were left after the gain, frame-quality and background checks (checks 1 to 3) and so went to the stacking engine. A large gap means many frames were set aside | The UI; people reading the summary |
| `framesExcludedForGain`, `framesExcludedForBackground` | Frames | None | Counts of the frames set aside by each check | The UI; people reading the summary |
| `framesQuarantined` | Frames | None | How many frames with clouds or trailed stars the frame-quality check moved into `_excluded`. A non-zero count adds a `stacking.notice_frames_moved_to_excluded` warning row, so a reader sees that the stack used fewer frames than the target holds | The UI; people reading the summary |
| `backgroundSplitDetected`, `backgroundSplitDetail` | Flag; text | None | Set when the frames fell into two sky-brightness groups. The detail gives each group's size and level | People reading the summary |
| `flatFrameCount`, `flatNoiseFraction`, `flatSmoothingSigmaPx` | Frames; fraction (0.005 is 0.5%); pixels | Noise 0.5% (partly measured; see [STK-05](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-05)) | The flat set's size, the master flat's relative noise, and the Gaussian width used to blur it, empty when none was applied. High noise means the blur is needed | The `flat_noise` gate; people reading the summary |
| `flatCalibrationIssues` | Sentences | None | One sentence per problem with the flats | The `flat_level` and `flat_noise` gates; the flat note warning row |
| `calibrationMismatchFlags` and the blocking flags | Sentences | None | One sentence per mismatch between the lights and the calibration frames. The library writes each sentence, naming the settings it applied and the lights it applied them to (for a dark, the lights' temperature, the master's temperature and the tolerance). A soft flag means the nearest calibration frames were still applied despite a gain, offset or temperature mismatch, and the `calibrationMismatchFlags` field holds these. A blocking flag means a kind of calibration frame was not applied at all, because its binning differs. Both kinds fail the `calibration_metadata` gate, which quotes every sentence | The `calibration_metadata` gate |
| `calibration_blocking_flags` | Sentences | Minimum 3 frames (design estimate; see [stacking design estimates](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#design-estimates)) | One sentence per master bias, dark or flat built from fewer frames than the minimum. They appear in the `calibration_frame_count` gate, and nothing else reads them | The `calibration_frame_count` gate |

## Files

- `frame_homogeneity.py`: the gain check.
- `frame_quarantine.py`: the frame-quality check and its limits.
- `blink_pair.py`: picks what the viewer's Blink button needs for a flagged or set-aside frame: a typical frame of the same session (stored star elongation and sky unevenness closest to the session medians) and one bright, isolated, unsaturated star near the center, with its position in both frames. The star comes from the star-shape measurement's candidate rules, and the position is mapped with the star matching of the pointing check, which allows for a meridian flip. It stores nothing. `QualityRecords.choose_blink_pair` and `QualityRecords.match_frame_positions` call it.
- `background_homogeneity.py`: the background check.
- `flat_calibration.py`: the flat check.
- `calibration_assessment.py`: judges a set of dark or bias frames from the numbers stored for each frame (cut off at zero, light on the sensor, level, noise, frame count and copies), and puts a flat set's check in the same form. It returns `GateResult` records and stores nothing.
- `dark_defect_judgement.py`: gives each dark of a set one verdict from its stored defect numbers (see `pipelines/shared/quality/dark_defect_measurement.py`): keep, keep with a warning (a long track inside the frame), or leave out (a track from one frame edge to another, or far more bright pixels than the set's other darks). The set must have 5 frames or more. It stores nothing; `dark_defect_run.py` writes the verdicts to the calibration library.
- `stack_calibration_health.py`: builds the stack's dark and bias gates, the missing-dark gate and the light temperature spread gate from the frames a stack used.
- `sensor_noise.py`: measures a camera's gain and read noise from pairs of flat frames and pairs of bias frames. It is not a step of the stack. The library call `calibration.measure_sensor_noise` uses it. Flat frames are paired by their `DATE-OBS` time, never across a gap of more than 300 s, and a flat above the camera profile's photometric linearity limit is refused. The difference of two frames taken one after the other holds only noise, and half its variance is the noise variance of one frame. Bias pairs give the read noise, and flat pairs give the gain (the bias-subtracted signal over the single-frame variance minus the read variance). It measures the central 1000 by 1000 pixels at one signal level, so it cannot show whether the sensor is linear. Its result matched a hand measurement (measured; see [STK-35](../../../../documentation/library_design/validation/stacking.md#stk-35)).
- `exposure_weighting.py`: the choice of per-frame weights for mixed exposure lengths.
- `stack_inputs.py`: the unchanged-stack record.
- `assess_input_quality.py`: the frame-count and calibration-match checks, and `record_input_counts`.

For exact behavior, read the code.
