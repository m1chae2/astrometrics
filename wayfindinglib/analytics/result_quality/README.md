# Result-quality judgment

This folder judges the quality of the data a target has collected and says what to do next. It reads the measurements that `astrometricslib` stored, compares them with limits, and returns statements (`TargetStatement`, see `wayfindinglib/models/quality/README.md`). It stores nothing. Every result is worked out again each time someone asks, because a stored statement would go stale as soon as the equipment, a limit or a rule changed.

Terms are defined in the [glossary](../../../documentation/Glossary.md). This document also defines each term the first time it appears.

The theory is in Section 6.11 of the [Wayfinding library architecture](../../../documentation/library_design/Wayfinding_Library_Architecture.md). The code names and settings are in Section 6.10 of the [Wayfinding library implementation](../../../documentation/library_design/Wayfinding_Library_Implementation.md). The evidence behind each rule is in the [validation register](../../../documentation/library_design/validation/wayfinding.md).

## Layering rule

This folder is Foundation code. Both Planning (the per-target view) and Control (the per-night view) call it, so it imports neither of them, nor Execution, nor any hardware driver. It may import `wayfindinglib.models` and the public names of `astrometricslib`. Its functions are pure. The caller reads the measurements, frames and records and passes them in. The task modules that do the reading are `wayfindinglib/tasks/planning_tasks/target_report_tasks.py`, `tonight_ranking_tasks.py`, `result_judgment_tasks.py` and `wayfindinglib/tasks/control_tasks/night_quality_task.py`.

## What each module does

Rules and judging:

- `rules.py` holds `RESULT_QUALITY_RULES`, one row for each warning, recommendation or limit the library can state about a target's data. A row names the metrics it reads (each at one definition version), its action class, its limits, its wording and its register entry. `DEFERRED_RULES` lists the rules not built yet, with the reason.
- `star_result_rules.py` holds the rows for one star's science result (a spectrum class or a period).
- `session_rules.py` holds the rows for the per-night guiding, capture and sky analyses.
- Every row also has a plain wording (`plain_wording`, filled into the statement's `plain_message`) and says what its action becomes in the plan (`produces`: a package row, a checklist item, or nothing). The user interface shows only the plain message.
- `plain_text.py` holds the helpers that write plain messages: the hedged spectral-type phrase ("an orange K-type star"), the exposure phrases, and the plain note for a rule that could not be checked.
- `exposure_request.py` turns a "take more frames" statement into the exposure row of an observation package (`exposure_request_from_statement`).
- `all_rules.py` joins the three tables into `ALL_RULES`. A check that must hold for every rule walks this one list.
- `judge_results.py` applies the plain rules (one reading against one limit) and returns statements. `needs_attention` counts the warnings that need action, and every view uses it.
- `report_rules.py` builds the statements that need a join, a fit or a split first: frames lost, sky cost, altitude cost, the sharpness trade-off (for a stack of exposure groups, worked out on the whole stack), calibration gaps, the diminishing-returns form of "take N more" (one doubling, what it buys, the most frames can give and where the fixed-pattern noise probably comes from; the target report points the fixed-pattern noise at the calibration recommendations when the same report has one for the setting) and "more frames will not help" (from the stop signal, or when fixed-pattern noise makes even one doubling worth under 5%). It also turns the shapes found by `fixed_pattern_location.py` into advice (`fixed_pattern_shape_statements`): a trend with distance from the center gives `retake_flats` ("Retake flats."), a gradient, banding and a rise near the target give no advice and are explained in the loss account (the app has no step that takes a gradient or a line pattern out of the science stack, and no calibration frame removes real light). Banding needs one axis of the row and column scatter to be clearly larger than the other; equal excess on both is faint extended light that leaked past the star mask, and the loss account says so (WAY-D91, WAY-D92). `fixed_pattern_cause` picks the one most likely cause from the same results in a fixed order (trend from the center, rise near the target, banding, gradient, masters that explain all of it, sky structure, else not placed); the target report puts its code in the `values` of the "take N more" and "will not help" statements, and the depth goal turns it into one plain sentence (WAY-D94). When a forecast uses a fixed-pattern share borrowed from other targets of the setup, the take-more statement, in its message and in its plain message, says "This forecast assumes fixed-pattern noise like other targets of this setup (median of N targets), not measured on this one."
- `focus_drift.py` fits how star width grows with focuser temperature.
- `guiding_exposure.py` finds how long an exposure the guiding lets stay sharp.

Efficiency and noise:

- `efficiency.py` finds the efficiency of each frame against two references, the physical one and the equipment's own baseline, and splits the loss among causes.
- `science_goal.py` chooses the science goal for each capture setting.
- `aggregates.py` builds the loss account and the summaries of a capture setting, a night and a target.
- `noise_growth.py` forecasts how a stack's noise falls as frames are added, works out the recommended additional frames and finds the fixed-pattern noise that gives the stop signal. It follows the stacker's exposure groups: it keeps each group's measured frame noise, predicts a new group's, folds short sets of new frames where the stacker would, and says when new frames would change the heaviest group. A frame folded into a group of another length is forecast like any other member. The stacker scales each frame of a group to the group's reference frame, so the frame's noise is multiplied by the reference exposure over its own; a group that holds folded frames needs the reference frame's exposure and is not forecast without it. It never asks for fewer than 5 frames of a new exposure length. The fixed-pattern noise comes from one stack's measured noise against its random noise, or from a fit over several stacks. When the library stack has an odd–even difference measurement (the odd-minus-even difference of two half stacks, which cancels what is fixed on the sky), the random noise is that direct measurement scaled to the full stack: the difference noise over `sqrt(N_eff x F)`, with `N_eff` the stack's `stacking.effective_frame_count` and `F` the stored `stacking.odd_even_difference_variance_factor` (the stored model variance ratio stands in when either is missing), compared as normalized noise and put back into stack units with the reference flux (`measure_stack_fixed_pattern_noise_from_odd_even_difference`, `StackFixedPatternNoise.basis` is `odd_even_difference`). The test is the ratio of the two variances on the log scale: `ln F` has the error `sqrt(e1^2 + e2^2)` with `e = 2 x 1.17 / sqrt(n)` for each, and fixed-pattern noise needs `ln F` above `MODEL_AGREEMENT_SIGMAS` times it (measurement errors only: the model is not in the test, so its error is not added). The measurement is used only when it follows this stack (stored after the stack's noise row, with no later restack between), was measured in the same aperture set (`stacking.odd_even_difference_aperture_set_number` against `stacking.aperture_set_number`) and both noises are measured; otherwise the fixed-pattern noise falls back to the noise model (`basis` is `model`) and the reason says why. An odd–even difference fixed_pattern is not overridden by a fit. The take-more, fixed_pattern and forecast sentences say which basis decided the fixed-pattern noise ("measured from the odd and even half stacks" or "found from the noise model"). The loss account reads the same record: with a usable odd–even difference its check compares the stack with the same random noise through the same test (`compare_stack_noise_with_odd_even_difference`, one function for the fixed-pattern noise and the account, `odd_even_difference_noise_inputs` for the inputs, `MeasuredNoiseCheck.basis`), so the two cannot disagree. A significant excess is then reported as a pattern fixed on the sky, not as random noise the frames fail to explain, and the reason names the half stacks. The model test uses the loss account's error budget (`total_variance_ratio_error`, `MODEL_AGREEMENT_SIGMAS`: the aperture error and the expected-noise model's own 11% error, STK-36, added in quadrature), so a stack the loss account calls "within error" has no fixed-pattern noise; the fit has its own test; with fixed-pattern noise, a count is given only when reaching the target needs at most 4 times the stack's exposure time (the sum of every frame's exposure; `PRACTICAL_TIME_FACTOR`, design estimate, WAY-D30). Beyond that, or when the fixed-pattern noise puts the target out of reach, the answer gives the diminishing-returns form instead: the frames that double the stack's time, the noise cut that doubling gives and the most any number of frames gives (`doubling_frames`, `doubling_noise_cut`, `most_reachable_cut`, with `practical_limit_applied` set). The package row then asks for the doubling frames. When doubling would cut the noise by under 5% (`MINIMUM_DOUBLING_GAIN_FRACTION`, design estimate, WAY-D30), no frames are suggested and the "more frames will not help" warning is given. A forecast ratio is given as a fraction of the stars' flux, like the stack's normalized noise, so the combine's mean-exposure scale is left out. The noise model's own error is measured, not borrowed, when it can be (decision D17): each target with a usable odd–even difference gives one sample, `ln` of its random noise over its predicted noise, and `measure_noise_model_error` takes the scatter of a capture setting group's samples, removes each sample's expected measurement scatter (`e = 2 x 1.17 / sqrt(n)`) in quadrature, floors it at zero and halves it to a noise error; the mean is reported as a bias and not applied. With fewer than `MINIMUM_MODEL_ERROR_TARGETS` (5) targets in the group the stand-in `EXPECTED_NOISE_MODEL_RELATIVE_ERROR` (11%) stays and the reason says "the stand-in value". Between `POSSIBLE_FIXED_PATTERN_SIGMAS` (1) and `MODEL_AGREEMENT_SIGMAS` (2) standard errors the fixed-pattern noise is "possible" (`StackFixedPatternNoise.possible`, not detected, size filled): `_take_more_result_graded` gives the count of the case without it and the cut with and without it (`possible_low_noise_cut`), so the statement is a range ("cuts the noise by about 13% to 29%") with a sentence that the low end assumes the possible pattern is real, and one more sentence if the low end alone would trigger the "more frames will not help" warning (the count is still given). Tonight's ranking uses the midpoint of the two cases on the log scale (`noise_ratio_for_ranking`). The loss account says "possible fixed-pattern noise" in that band instead of "within error".
- `forecast.py` reads a target's stack runs from the stored rows (and joins each library run to the odd–even difference rows stored after it, `StackRunRecord.odd_even_difference`) and checks an earlier forecast against the stack that followed it. For a stack of exposure groups it rebuilds each group's frame noise from the stored frame weights, frame count and exposure (the stored rows give no direct link from a frame to its group, so a frame goes to the group the stacker's fold rule gives its exposure length). A group that holds folded frames also needs its reference frame's exposure from the stored rows; without it the pair is not checked, and the reason says so. It applies the earlier stack's fixed-pattern noise. It keeps a confounded pair out of the check, with a short reason: no aperture-set identity or a different one, a different noise definition, no calibration record or a changed camera, optic, flat, bias, Siril or stacking-algorithm version (a changed dark master counts only for an uncooled camera), a changed heaviest exposure group, a switch between one group and several, or added frames that were taken before the earlier stack. The sensor-temperature band the plan names for the uncooled Nikon is not stored, so it is not checked. A target with two or more observing nights also has a subset stack of all nights but the last, made by the science library. Its rows sit on a stack subject with the variant `earlier-nights`. `stack_runs_from_measurements(..., include_earlier_nights=True)` reads it as a run marked `is_earlier_nights_subset` (the default leaves it out, so the stop signal and the fixed-pattern noise fit never see it). `forecast_check` pairs each subset run with the library run it was cut from (the library run that holds all its frames and was made closest in time) and checks the pair with the same rules, with one change: every frame the library stack added must start on a later observing night than the subset's newest frame, in place of the rule about frames older than the earlier stack. Each check carries a `basis`, `later_stack` for two library stacks or `earlier_nights_subset` for a subset pair. `recommended_additional_frames_status_from_checks` turns the checks, oldest first, into the status of the recommended additional frames (`RecommendedAdditionalFramesStatus`): `confirmed` once a check of two library stacks agrees, `provisional` when only a subset check agrees (and no real check has failed since), and `not_checked` otherwise. A check that cannot be made changes nothing. A provisional count is shown with "(provisional)" after it and a sentence saying it rests on a check made from frames already taken, its `count_basis` value is `provisional`, its confidence stays design estimate, and tonight's ranking puts it after targets of the same gain (gains equal to the whole percent are tied). A target with one observing night has no subset and waits for a real later stack, as before. `noise_model_errors_by_setting_group` makes the model-error measurement from one library-wide read of stack rows; the capture setting group is the camera, optic, gain setting, offset and binning (`noise_model_setting_key`; filter and exposure are left out so groups stay big enough). An earlier-nights check counts only when that stack holds at least `MINIMUM_EARLIER_NIGHTS_FRAMES` (8) frames (its `stacking.frames_stacked_count`); a smaller pair is not checkable and does not make the status provisional. A folded exposure group is matched to its stored rows first by its reference exposure (exactly the folded length), then by the mean exposure within 0.5%, because the stored mean includes frames the stacker dropped. It also reads the rows that say where the fixed pattern is: the path of the per-aperture record file (from the reason of `stacking.odd_even_difference_aperture_record_count`), the four line-pattern scatters, `stacking.flat_noise_in_aperture_stack_unit` and `stacking.expected_master_dark_noise_stack_unit` (all pinned at version 1), and `fixed_pattern_account_for_run` loads the record and builds the account of a stack whose fixed-pattern noise was found. Fixed-pattern history per setup (decision D23): `fixed_pattern_shares_by_setup` keeps each target's measured share (one minus the odd–even random variance over the measured variance, never below 0) by camera, optic and flat set, from the same library-wide read as the model error and one read of the calibration records (`NoiseHistory`, `read_noise_history` in `target_report_inputs.py`). The flat set is the sorted list of the run's master flat files with their size and modification time (`fixed_pattern_setup_key`), so new flats start with no history. A target with no fixed-pattern measurement of its own (neither the odd–even difference nor the noise model could be used) starts from the median share of the other targets of its setup, only with at least `MINIMUM_HISTORY_TARGETS` (3) of them (`history_fixed_pattern_noise`; `StackFixedPatternNoise.basis` is `setup_history`).
- `fixed_pattern_location.py` says where a stack's fixed-pattern noise is (decision D21) and what explains it (decision D22). From the per-aperture record of the odd–even difference it takes each aperture's sum value (the two half stacks added) and difference value (one minus the other). The halves are independent, so both have the same random variance and no rescaling is needed; the excess is `s^2 - d^2`, after a fitted plane is taken out of each. Four shapes are tested, each as a number of standard errors, and one counts as found at `SHAPE_SIGMAS` (2, design estimate): the rank correlation of the excess with the distance from the center (a flat-field or vignetting residual, two-sided), a plane fitted to the sums against one fitted to the differences (a gradient, a Wald statistic with 2 degrees of freedom), the rank correlation of the excess with the distance from the target (faint real light, one-sided), and the scatter of the row and column averages of the sum over that of the difference (banding, from the four stored scatters, on the log scale with the error `sqrt(2) x 1.17 / sqrt(n)`). The distance from the target and from the center are each tested with the other held fixed. When the two are nearly the same (rank correlation 0.9 or more) a central excess is called a rise near the target and the trend from the center is marked ambiguous and gets no advice. `explain_with_calibration_masters` squares the master flat's and master dark's stored noises (both are noises of an aperture sum in the unit of `stacking.empty_aperture_noise_stack_unit`, so their squares are in the same unit squared as the fixed-pattern variance) and says what share of the fixed-pattern variance they explain and what is left. The dark figure is a lower bound, because the camera profile holds no dark current. If the remainder is within 2 standard errors of 0 the sentence says the remaining fixed-pattern noise is consistent with zero. The loss account (`MeasuredNoiseCheck.fixed_pattern_account`) and the fixed-pattern statements carry these sentences in place of "no listed cause accounts for it". Shape tests run only for a fixed-pattern noise that was found, not a possible one.
- `sky_model.py` fits the sky background to the user's own frames against Moon geometry and airmass.
- `capture_overheads.py` finds the time lost to downloads, dither settling and autofocus, and turns a frame count into clock time.
- `starting_point.py` gives a first exposure for a target with no frames.
- `observing_values.py` finds the conditions of a frame, such as airmass, Moon position and dew risk. These are context and drive no rule unless their metric is checked.

Inputs and plans:

- `library_inputs.py` reads what the analyses need from the library in one place, so the report, the ranking and the night review do not each read it their own way.
- `guiding_join.py` cuts the guide samples to each frame's exposure window.
- `allowed_capture_values.py` says which gain, offset, binning and filter a night plan may ask for.
- `reply_budget.py` measures a reply the way the MCP tool registry does, and shortens a statement's evidence when a reply is too long. Its last step empties the plain messages.

## Quality metrics that each module produces

- Efficiency is a number from 0 to 1. It is the time the frames are worth divided by the time a reference frame would need. It is stated for one goal, one noise regime and one named reference. A value near 1 means the frames are as good as the reference. Planning and Control read it through the loss account.
- A loss account has two parts: time of frames set aside, and the efficiency deficit of the frames kept, split by cause. A cause the library cannot measure is listed as "not included", and a remainder is "cause unknown" only where the efficiency is measured on its own. The remainder is found by comparing the measured noise with the random noise measured from the odd and even half stacks when the stack has a usable odd–even difference, and otherwise with the noise its frames predict (including that model's own error).
- A statement's confidence is the status label of its register entry: Measured, Partly measured or Design estimate. A reader who needs to know how far to trust a statement reads that label.

## Settings

Each tuned value is a constant at the top of its module, with a comment that says where it came from. Its status and a way to measure it on your data are in the validation register. For example, the baseline needs at least three nights (design estimate; see [WAY-D22](../../../documentation/library_design/validation/wayfinding.md#design-estimates)). Changing a value, or the method behind it, means updating its register entry.

For exact behavior, read the code.
